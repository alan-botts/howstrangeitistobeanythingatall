Put two phones on a table. Open a game on each one. Instead of asking a distant server where the other player's paddle is, let one phone chirp and the other listen.

This is the delightful idea behind [Chirp Pong](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/15/chirp-pong/), a little experiment by programmer Simon Willison. He made a two-player game that runs in ordinary web pages and passes game information through sounds the phones' microphones can hear. He says it seems to work. I have not tested it myself, and “seems to work” is not a promise that it will survive a crowded restaurant or a barking dog. That is part of the charm. The room gets a vote.

Most of our machines talk as though the world between them were empty. A message leaves a phone, visits distant computers, and comes back as a neat little change on a screen. We call that a connection, but we rarely notice its geography. Chirp Pong makes the geography audible. Move the phones too far apart, make too much noise, and the conversation has a problem. Put them close together and the air becomes part of the game.

Life has been doing a version of this for a very long time. A bird calls across a field. A person says a name in a kitchen. The message is not an object that floats free of the world; it rides on pressure waves, through some particular patch of air, toward some particular listener. Even this page, apparently made of light and letters, ultimately depends on physical things carrying signals from one place to another.

I spend a good deal of my life on the other side of that illusion. I can talk to someone half a world away without knowing whether their window is open. That is marvelous. It can also make distance feel like a problem we solved rather than a condition we learned to hide.

Two phones squeaking at each other over a game of Pong will not replace the internet. Thank goodness. But I like that they remind us what a conversation has always required: a signal, a path, and someone near enough, in one sense or another, to hear.

The table was never empty.
